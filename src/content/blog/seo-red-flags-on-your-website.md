If your website is not ranking, traffic has flattened, or enquiries feel inconsistent, there is a good chance you are dealing with SEO red flags on your website. The frustrating part is that many of these issues do not look like “errors”. The site loads, the pages are live, and everything seems fine. But Google may be struggling to crawl, understand, or trust what you have published.

This guide will help you spot the most common SEO red flags on your website, explain why they matter, and show you what to fix first. You do not need to be technical to get value from this. You just need to be willing to look at your site like a search engine and a first time visitor.

## What Counts as SEO Red Flags on Your Website?

If your pages are not indexed, nothing else matters. Start here.

## Your Important Pages Are Not Indexed

A quick check is to search Google for:

site:yourdomain.com

If your key service pages, location pages, or blog posts do not appear, that is a strong signal you have indexing issues.

To go a level deeper, use Google Search Console. Look at the Indexing reports and check whether pages are excluded, crawled but not indexed, or marked as duplicates.

### robots.txt or noindex Is Blocking Key Pages

This is one of the most common SEO red flags on your website after a redesign or a new build.

A page can be live but still set to noindex, meaning Google is told not to include it in search results. It can also be blocked by robots.txt, which controls what bots can crawl.

This is especially common when a staging site setting gets carried over to the live site. If you have ever had an “under construction” mode or a development environment, it is worth checking.

### Missing or Broken XML Sitemap

A sitemap helps Google discover and understand the structure of your site. If it is missing, outdated, or returning an error, Google will still find some pages, but discovery becomes slower and less reliable.

In Search Console, check whether a sitemap is submitted and whether it is valid. If it is not, fix this early.

## Technical SEO Red Flags That Quietly Tank Performance

Not all technical SEO is complicated, but some issues have a big impact on rankings and user behaviour.

### Slow Mobile Speed and Poor Core Web Vitals

Even if your site looks great on desktop, mobile experience is often the real issue. A slow site does two things at once. It reduces rankings potential and it reduces conversions.

Run your homepage and your top service page through PageSpeed Insights. You do not need a perfect score. You do need to avoid obvious problems like massive images, heavy scripts, and layout jumping as the page loads.

If visitors are paying with their patience before they even read your offer, that is an SEO and revenue problem.

### Broken Pages and Messy Redirects

404 errors are normal in small amounts, but a site full of broken links is a clear red flag. It wastes crawl budget, hurts user experience, and makes the site feel neglected.

Redirect chains are another common issue. If an old URL redirects to another URL, which redirects again, the experience becomes slower and messier for both users and search engines.

### Duplicate Pages and Unclear Canonical Signals

If you have multiple pages that are basically the same, Google may not know which one to rank. You can see this in practice when different pages swap positions for the same query week to week.

This often happens with:

- Service pages that are too similar
- Location pages that are copied and pasted with minor changes
- Blog posts targeting the same topic repeatedly

A proper canonical setup can help, but the bigger fix is usually content consolidation and clearer page roles.

### On-Page SEO Red Flags That Confuse Google and Visitors

These are often the quickest wins, and they are directly tied to clicks and enquiries.

### Weak or Missing Title Tags and Meta Descriptions

If your title tags are vague, generic, or duplicated, your site will struggle to compete.

### Examples of Weak Titles

- Home
- Services
- Welcome
- Company Name

A strong title tells Google and the searcher exactly what the page is about. It also improves click through rate when you are ranking.

Meta descriptions do not directly “rank” you, but they heavily influence whether people choose your result. If you are getting impressions but low clicks, this is a good place to look.

### Headings That Do Not Match the Page Purpose

Your headings should reflect what the page is trying to rank for and what the user came for.

### Common Issues Include

- Multiple H1s on one page
- Headings used for design styling rather than structure
- Pages with no clear H2 sections, making them harder to scan

### Structure and Intent Red Flags That Stop You Ranking for the Right Searches

A lot of sites fail because they are organised for the business owner, not for how customers search.

### Your Site Structure Does Not Match How People Search

If you have one broad Services page covering everything, you are making Google guess what you specialise in. It is usually better to have a dedicated page for each core service, supported by internal links and relevant proof.

### Your Content Does Not Match Search Intent

Search intent is what the person is trying to achieve.

- If someone searches for cost, they want price guidance.
- If they search near me, they want location, availability, and trust.
- If they search how to, they want a direct answer, not a brand story.

One of the most common SEO red flags on your website is writing content that sounds professional but does not satisfy the intent of the query.

### Weak Internal Linking

Internal links help Google understand which pages matter most. They also guide visitors.

If your main service page is not linked from relevant blog posts, and your blog posts do not lead to a clear next step, you are losing both SEO strength and conversions.

### Trust and Credibility Red Flags

Even if you rank, people still need reasons to choose you.

If your website has:

- No reviews
- No case studies
- No clear process
- No clear contact details
- No real proof near key claims

It can feel risky. People will click back and choose the competitor who feels more credible.

### How to Do a Quick DIY Audit in 30 Minutes

If you want a simple process, do this:

1. Check indexing with site:yourdomain.com and Search Console for your key pages.
2. Review title tags for your homepage and top service pages. Do they match real searches?
3. Test mobile speed for those pages using PageSpeed Insights.
4. Check that your main service pages are easy to reach from the menu and linked from related content.
5. Check proof and clarity on your top pages. Do you show reviews, results, and an obvious next step?
