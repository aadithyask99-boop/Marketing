For years, Google Search Console was something you opened to check how your website was performing. You looked at clicks, impressions, search queries, and technical issues. Your social media analytics lived somewhere else, inside Instagram, YouTube, TikTok, or X.

That has changed.

Google now allows you to connect supported social media accounts directly to Search Console through Platform Properties. This gives businesses a new way to understand how their social content is discovered through Google Search.

If you have been wondering how to track social media performance in Google Search Console, this guide explains what has changed, why it matters, and how you can use the data to improve both your content and your visibility.

![How to Track Social Media Performance in Google Search Console](/blog-media/track-social-media-performance-in-google-search-console-1.webp)

## Why this update matters

Search and social media have slowly been moving closer together for years.

People no longer discover businesses through websites alone. They find Instagram posts, YouTube videos, TikTok clips, and X posts directly from Google Search.

That means your social content is no longer only competing inside social platforms. It is also competing in search.

Understanding how to track social media performance in Google Search Console helps you see which social posts are appearing in Google, which search queries are driving traffic, and which topics deserve more attention.

It is another piece of the puzzle when building a stronger digital presence.

![How do I set up social media tracking in Search Console](/blog-media/track-social-media-performance-in-google-search-console-2.webp)

## What are Platform Properties?

Platform Properties are a new property type inside Google Search Console.

Instead of adding only websites, you can now add supported social media platforms and monitor how their public content performs within Google Search.

At the moment, Google supports:

- Instagram
- YouTube
- TikTok
- X

Each platform becomes its own property inside Search Console, with its own reports and performance data.

It is important to understand that Search Console is not replacing Instagram Insights or YouTube Analytics.

Instead, it answers a different question:

"How are people finding my social content through Google?"

## What data can you see?

![How to Track Social Media Performance in Google Search Console](/blog-media/track-social-media-performance-in-google-search-console-3.webp)

Once your account has been verified, Search Console provides three useful reports.

### Performance Report

This shows:

- Clicks
- Impressions
- Average click through rate
- Average position

You can also filter the data to see which posts are generating traffic from Google Search, Discover, and Google News where applicable.

This is probably the most useful report because it connects search behaviour with individual pieces of content.

### Insights Report

The Insights report gives a broader overview.

It highlights:

- Recent traffic trends
- Top performing posts
- How people discover your content

Instead of looking through hundreds of rows of data, you quickly see what is working.

### Achievements

Search Console now celebrates milestones based on search performance.

For example, reaching a certain number of clicks from Google Search over the last 28 days.

While this is not the most important report, it provides a simple way to monitor long term growth.

### How to track social media performance in Google Search Console

Setting everything up only takes a few minutes.

1. Open Google Search Console.
2. Click the property selector.
3. Select "Add property".
4. Choose one of the supported social platforms.
5. Complete the verification process.
6. Wait a few days while Google starts collecting data.

Each social account needs to be added separately.

Once verified, you will begin seeing search performance for that individual platform.

If you cannot see Platform Properties yet, do not worry. Google is rolling the feature out gradually.

## How to Track Social Media Performance in Google Search Console

Follow these simple steps to connect your social media profiles with Google Search Console and start monitoring their search visibility and performance.

| Step | What You Need to Do |
| --- | --- |
| 1. Open Google Search Console | Sign in to your Google Search Console account using the Google account that manages your website or business. |
| 2. Click the Property Selector | Select the property dropdown located in the top-left corner of the dashboard to manage or add a new property. |
| 3. Select "Add Property" | Click **Add Property** to begin adding a new website or supported social media property for tracking. |
| 4. Choose Your Social Platform | Select one of the supported social media platforms or property types you want Google Search Console to monitor. |
| 5. Complete Verification | Verify ownership using Google's recommended verification method to activate performance tracking. |
| 6. Allow Data Collection | Wait a few days while Google gathers search performance data. Once processed, you'll be able to monitor impressions, clicks, indexing, and other valuable SEO insights. |

### What to do with the data

The data becomes valuable when you stop treating every post as a one-off.

Suppose Search Console shows that people keep finding one of your Instagram posts by searching:

"how to improve website speed"

Instead of moving on to the next trend, you could build an entire content cluster around that topic.

For example:

- a Reel explaining the issue
- a carousel with practical tips
- a blog answering the question in depth
- a service page mentioning speed optimisation
- an FAQ section addressing common concerns

One search query becomes multiple pieces of useful content.

That is far more sustainable than constantly asking, "What should we post today?"

### Search behaviour is changing

For a long time, SEO and social media were treated as separate disciplines.

SEO focused on websites.

Social focused on engagement.

That separation is becoming less useful.

Google now indexes more social content.

AI search engines increasingly understand content across multiple platforms.

Customers move between Google, Instagram, YouTube, ChatGPT, and back again before making a decision.

Businesses that connect those touchpoints will have a stronger digital presence than businesses that treat every platform as its own island.

### Common mistakes to avoid

One mistake is focusing only on likes and followers.

A post with modest engagement inside Instagram may still perform well in Google Search.

Another mistake is creating content based only on trends.

Trending audio disappears.

Questions people search for often remain relevant for years.

Finally, many businesses collect data but never use it.

The value of learning how to track social media performance in Google Search Console is not the dashboard itself.

It is what you do after reading the dashboard.

If a topic keeps appearing in search, create more content around it.

If older content starts receiving new traffic, update it rather than abandoning it.

Let the data shape your content strategy.
