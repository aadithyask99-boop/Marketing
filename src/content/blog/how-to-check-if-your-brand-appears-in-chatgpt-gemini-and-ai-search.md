A potential customer asks ChatGPT to recommend a product, supplier or local service. It gives them three businesses to consider.

Is yours one of them?

That is becoming an important question for businesses as people use ChatGPT, Gemini, Google AI search and other AI tools to research options before they buy.

The good news is that you can check.

In this guide, we will show you **how to check if your brand appears in ChatGPT, Gemini and AI search**, what questions to test, what to record and what to look at if your competitors keep appearing while you do not.

If you are new to the subject, start with our guide to [what AI search visibility is and how to measure it](/blogs/what-is-ai-search-visibility-how-do-you-measure-it/).

## What does it actually mean to appear in AI search?

There are a few different ways your business can appear.

**Brand recognition:** AI knows who your business is and can explain what you do when someone asks about you directly.

**Brand mention:** Your business appears in an answer about a relevant product, service or subject.

**Citation:** An AI answer uses a page from your website as one of its sources.

**Recommendation:** Your business is actively suggested as an option for someone considering a purchase or service.

There is an important difference between these.

If you ask ChatGPT, “What does [your business name] do?” and it answers correctly, that tells you the platform knows something about your company.

But a potential customer who has never heard of you is unlikely to search that way.

The more valuable question is whether your business appears when people ask about the **problem you solve, the category you operate in or the options they should consider**.

## Start with the questions your customers might actually ask

Before checking ChatGPT or Gemini, think about what someone might ask before choosing a business like yours.

Do not test only your company name.

Build a small list covering different stages of the buying decision.

For example, a UK business could test questions such as:

Start with around 10 to 20 questions that genuinely matter to your business.

## How to check if your brand appears in ChatGPT, Gemini and AI search

Once you have your questions, test the same set across the platforms most relevant to your customers.

You could start with:

- ChatGPT
- Google Gemini
- Perplexity
- Google AI search experiences

Run each question and look beyond whether your company name appears.

**Was your brand mentioned?**

If yes, look at the context. Was the business simply listed, or was it described as a relevant option?

**Was your business recommended?**

A recommendation is more commercially useful than a passing mention.

**Was your website cited?**

If AI uses your website as a source, note which page it selected.

**Which competitors appeared?**

Sometimes the most useful information is not that you are missing, but seeing which businesses appear instead.

**Is the information accurate?**

Check your services, locations, products and other details. Outdated or incorrect information is worth identifying even when the brand appears.

**What does AI associate your business with?**

You may find that AI understands one part of your company very well but barely connects you with another important service or product.

Do not judge your visibility based on a single response. AI-generated answers can vary, so consistent checking is more useful than one screenshot.

Keep the same core questions and repeat the test regularly.

Over time, patterns become much more useful than individual results.

You might discover that your educational content is regularly cited, but your business is rarely recommended commercially. Or you might be visible on one AI platform but almost absent from another.

That gives you something specific to investigate.

## Can AI visibility checker tools help?

Yes. Manual checking is useful because you control the questions and can make them specific to your customers.

Third-party tools can provide another view.

The [Ahrefs AI Visibility Checker](https://ahrefs.com/ai-visibility-checker) can help you check where a brand appears across AI platforms and identify topics and sources connected with that visibility.

The [Semrush AI Search Visibility Checker](https://www.semrush.com/free-tools/ai-search-visibility-checker/) can also provide a broader view of AI mentions, cited pages and the topics associated with a brand.

These tools can be useful for broader checks, but they should not be treated as a perfect record of every AI answer every customer sees.

Your own prompt set still matters because it reflects the questions that are commercially important to your business.

## What if your competitors appear and you do not?

This is where the check becomes useful.

Simply knowing that you are missing does not solve anything. The next job is understanding why.

### Check whether your website clearly explains what you do

Your main products, services, locations and areas of expertise should be easy for both people and search systems to understand.

Poor website structure, thin service pages or important information buried across unrelated pages can make this harder.

### Look at the questions your content answers

If customers regularly ask a question but your website has nothing useful about it, there is little for an AI system to discover, understand or cite.

Our guide to [how to build content AI actually understands](/blogs/how-to-build-content-ai-actually-understands/) goes deeper into structuring useful content around real customer questions.

### Look beyond your own website

What other sources connect your brand with the subject?

Reviews, industry publications, directories, case studies, community discussions and credible third-party mentions all contribute to the wider information available about a business.

Read [Why Brand Mentions Matter More Than Backlinks](/blogs/why-brand-mentions-matter-more-than-backlinks/) to understand why the context around those mentions matters.

### Review your technical foundations

Website structure, internal linking, crawlability and relevant schema markup can make it easier for search systems to understand relationships between your business, services, locations and content.

### Ask whether there is enough evidence to recommend you

Being understood and being recommended are not the same thing.

AI systems need enough useful information and supporting signals to confidently include a business when someone asks for options.

Our guide to [how to get your business recommended by ChatGPT](/blogs/how-to-get-your-business-recommended-by-chatgpt/) covers this in more detail.

We have seen the value of building these foundations with iAudit Global too. Its wider organic strategy included structured website pages and search-led content, and the visibility tracked later included thousands of AI citations.

You can read the full [iAudit Global organic growth case study](/blogs/iaudit-global-organic-growth/) to see how search-led content, website structure and wider authority worked together.

## Checking once is not enough

Think of your first check as a baseline.

Keep a core group of questions that matter to your business and revisit them monthly. Look for changes in mentions, citations, recommendations, competitor visibility and accuracy.

You can then connect those manual checks with the wider measurement methods covered in our [guide to measuring AI search visibility](/blogs/what-is-ai-search-visibility-how-do-you-measure-it/).

The aim is not to chase every change in an AI answer.

It is to understand whether your business is becoming easier to find for the questions that could genuinely lead to a customer.
