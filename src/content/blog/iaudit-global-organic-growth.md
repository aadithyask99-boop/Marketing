## The challenge

The product had a clear purpose. The market was highly specialised, and so was the audience. The website needed to speak to auditors, quality professionals, compliance teams, organisations running several sites, and the decision-makers looking for a more structured way to manage audit programmes.

That made this bigger than a website. We had to:

- Build the digital presence from the ground up.
- Understand a technically complex subject.
- Work out how potential customers were actually searching for solutions.
- Build visibility across search engines and newer AI-driven discovery.

And there was no paid acquisition budget driving results. Everything had to be earned organically, so it had to compound.

## The approach: research before design

We started with research, not design. We studied the audit and compliance landscape, the terminology the industry really uses, competing platforms, search behaviour and the questions people ask.

We also looked past the obvious commercial keywords. The opportunity was in the questions, problems and topics around internal audits, ISO standards, audit management, compliance and corrective actions. If the site could answer those, it could meet people at every stage of their research, not only at the moment they were ready to buy.

## Building the website from scratch

The iAudit website was planned as a large, structured resource rather than a product brochure. It has grown to 80+ pages across the core product, individual ISO standards, industries, company information and supporting content.

Dedicated pages for each standard and industry let the site answer specific search intent instead of forcing one product page to say everything. The structure also leaves room to grow as the business moves into new topics, markets and audiences.

### A measurement setup from the start

The site was supported by a complete tracking setup: Google Analytics, Google Tag Manager, Google Search Console, Microsoft Bing Webmaster Tools, Microsoft Clarity and Meta Pixel. It shows how people find the site, what they do on it and where the experience can improve.

## Organic search performance

The results began to compound as the website, content and technical foundation matured. In Search Console's 12-month view, the site recorded 558 organic clicks and 8.98k impressions, with a 6.2% average CTR and an average position of 17.9. In one three-month period it recorded 455 clicks and 7,030 impressions.

## Visibility beyond Google

Search was only one part of the plan. As AI tools became part of how people find information, we looked at where the information behind those systems comes from, and how iAudit could build a wider footprint.

That meant relevant Reddit communities and a dedicated iAudit subreddit, community participation, Medium, Substack and LinkedIn, including LinkedIn Articles and a newsletter. Reddit mattered in particular, because discussions and answers there can surface in search results and are widely used as reference material.

## Building iAudit's LinkedIn presence from zero

We treated LinkedIn as an acquisition channel in its own right, not somewhere to repost website content. We built and managed the page from scratch, with content around the problems, standards and questions its professional audience cares about. Over about 12 months it reached 872 organic followers, with no paid follower campaigns.

## Global reach

The approach was designed for a global SaaS audience from day one. The website received impressions from 129 countries, far beyond its original operating base. For a specialised B2B product, that spread is a useful sign the content and search strategy are reaching different markets rather than staying in one region.

But the most important result is the foundation. iAudit was not built around a short-term traffic campaign. The architecture, content library, technical SEO, community activity and social presence were built to keep generating visibility as the brand grows.

## A long-term organic growth strategy

Building authority in a niche B2B category takes time. There are fewer searches than in broad consumer markets, the audiences are more specialised, and buying decisions often involve research across several touchpoints. So we built those touchpoints one by one.

The website is the foundation. SEO brings relevant searches in. Content answers the questions behind those searches. LinkedIn keeps the brand in front of its professional audience. Community activity creates visibility outside the site. And AI-focused distribution helps the brand show up in newer discovery channels.

The work is still ongoing. The aim is not a temporary spike in traffic, but a digital presence that becomes more useful, more visible and more authoritative over time.

**Built slowly. Built properly. Built for the long term.**
