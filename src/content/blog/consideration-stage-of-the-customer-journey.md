When people first realise they have a problem, they rarely make an immediate purchase. Instead, they spend time understanding their options, comparing businesses and deciding which solution is most likely to meet their needs. This period of research is known as the **Consideration Stage of the Customer Journey**.

For businesses, this stage is significant because it often determines whether a potential customer continues towards a purchase or chooses a competitor. The information people find during their research shapes their perception of a business long before they speak to a sales team or request a quote.

[If you have already read our guide to the Awareness Stage, you'll know that customers first recognise a problem before they begin searching for solutions.](/blogs/awareness-stage-of-the-customer-journey/) This article continues that journey by exploring what happens next, how customers behave during the consideration stage and what businesses can do to build confidence before a buying decision is made.

## What Is the Consideration Stage of the Customer Journey?

The Consideration Stage of the Customer Journey begins once a customer understands they have a problem or objective. Instead of asking, "What is wrong?", they begin asking, "What is the best way to solve it?"

At this point, customers are not committed to a particular business or service. They are gathering information, comparing different approaches and narrowing their choices before making a final decision.

For example, imagine a business owner notices that their website receives plenty of visitors but very few enquiries. They may start researching possible solutions such as a [website redesign, search engine optimisation, paid advertising, conversion rate optimisation or AI powered customer support.](/our-services/) Rather than selecting the first company they find, they are likely to compare several providers, review their experience and assess which solution best fits their goals.

This stage is about evaluation. Customers are looking for evidence that a business understands their challenges and can provide an appropriate solution.

## How the Consideration Stage Fits into the Customer Journey

Every customer journey follows a series of stages, each with a different purpose.

### Awareness

The awareness stage begins when customers identify a problem or recognise an opportunity for improvement. Their priority is understanding what they are experiencing rather than choosing a product or service.

If you have not already read it, [our guide to the Awareness Stage explains how businesses can attract potential customers before they begin comparing solutions.](/blogs/awareness-stage-of-the-customer-journey/)

### Consideration

[During the consideration stage, customers actively evaluate their options.](https://contentsquare.com/blog/consideration-stage-customer-journey/) They compare businesses, services, pricing, expertise and previous work while deciding which providers deserve a place on their shortlist.

Their research often includes reading blog articles, exploring service pages, watching demonstrations and looking for customer reviews.

## Awareness Stage vs. Consideration Stage

Trust is one of the biggest deciding factors for customers during the **awareness stage of the customer journey**. Before contacting your business, people will often visit your website, read reviews and compare you with competitors.

Ask yourself a few simple questions.

Does your branding look professional?

Is your messaging consistent?

Can visitors quickly understand what your business does?

Does your website give them confidence to get in touch?

These are often the factors that influence a customer's decision far more than a discount or promotional offer.

### Decision

Once customers have gathered enough information, they move to the decision stage. They choose the business they believe offers the best solution and take the next step, whether that is requesting a proposal, booking a consultation or making a purchase.

The quality of information provided during the consideration stage often influences whether a business reaches this final stage.

## How Customers Behave During the Consideration Stage

Customers become much more deliberate during this phase of the buying journey. Their decisions are based on research rather than impulse.

### They research multiple solutions

Most customers explore several possible solutions before making contact with a business. They read industry blogs, search Google, watch videos, browse social media and seek recommendations from colleagues or friends.

Someone looking to improve their online presence may compare website redesigns with search engine optimisation, paid advertising or automation tools before deciding which approach is most appropriate.

This is why educational content is so valuable. Businesses that explain different options clearly are more likely to earn trust than those focused solely on promoting themselves.

### They compare businesses rather than products alone

Customers rarely compare prices in isolation.

Instead, they look at factors such as experience, previous projects, customer feedback, response times, technical expertise and the overall quality of communication.

A business with clear service information, transparent processes and relevant examples often creates a stronger impression than one that provides very little detail.

[Detailed service pages also help customers understand exactly what is included before they make an enquiry.](/our-services/)

### They look for evidence

Customers want reassurance that a business has successfully solved similar problems before.

They often read testimonials, explore portfolios, review case studies and search for measurable outcomes. Evidence helps reduce uncertainty and makes it easier to compare different providers.

For example, if a company is considering a website redesign, they are likely to examine previous projects to understand the standard of work they can expect. If they are researching SEO or digital marketing, they may look for examples that demonstrate improved visibility, stronger engagement or increased enquiries.

### They ask more detailed questions

As customers become more confident in their shortlist, their questions become more specific.

They may ask:

- How long will the project take?
- What is included in the service?
- Will this solution work for my business?
- What level of support is available after launch?
- How will success be measured?

Businesses that answer these questions clearly help customers make informed decisions without unnecessary uncertainty.

### What Content Helps Customers During the Consideration Stage?

The type of content customers look for changes once they enter the consideration stage. They are no longer trying to understand the problem. Instead, they want reliable information that helps them compare solutions and make a well informed decision.

Businesses that provide useful, transparent content are more likely to remain on a customer's shortlist throughout this process.

### Comparison articles

Comparison articles help customers evaluate different products, services or approaches side by side. Rather than promoting a single option, they explain the strengths, limitations and ideal use cases for each.

For example, a business deciding how to improve its online presence might compare SEO with Google Ads or weigh the benefits of redesigning an existing website against building a new one. Articles like these allow customers to choose a solution that matches their goals instead of relying on assumptions.

### Buyer's guides

A buyer's guide brings together the information customers need before making a purchase. It explains what to look for, the questions to ask and the factors that should influence a decision.

For more complex services, a buyer's guide can help customers understand topics such as project timelines, expected outcomes, pricing considerations and the level of support they should expect.

### Case studies

Case studies show how a business has solved similar challenges for previous clients. They provide context by explaining the problem, the solution that was implemented and the results that followed.

This type of content helps customers understand what a service looks like in practice and whether it is likely to meet their own requirements.

### Product demonstrations

Product demonstrations give customers a closer look at how a product or service works before they commit.

Whether presented as a video, live presentation or interactive walkthrough, demonstrations help customers understand features, functionality and the overall user experience. They also answer practical questions that may not be covered in written content.

### Webinars

Webinars allow businesses to explore a topic in greater depth while giving customers the opportunity to learn directly from experienced professionals.

They are particularly useful for explaining more technical services, answering common questions and demonstrating expertise in a practical setting.

### Frequently Asked Questions (FAQs)

A well structured FAQ section addresses the questions customers are most likely to ask during the consideration stage.

Topics such as pricing, timelines, project processes, ongoing support and deliverables can often be answered before a customer even gets in touch. This reduces uncertainty and helps people move forward with greater confidence.

### Expert reviews

Independent reviews and professional opinions can carry significant weight during the decision making process.

Customers often seek third party perspectives to validate their own research. Positive reviews from respected industry professionals or trusted publications can strengthen credibility and provide additional reassurance.

### Customer testimonials

Testimonials offer a direct insight into previous clients' experiences.

Rather than focusing solely on results, effective testimonials explain how a business communicated, solved problems and supported the client throughout the project. This helps prospective customers understand what working with the business is actually like.

### Free trials and interactive demonstrations

Whenever possible, giving customers the opportunity to experience a product or service before purchasing can reduce hesitation.

A free trial, interactive demo or consultation allows people to evaluate whether the solution meets their expectations before making a financial commitment.

### Checklists and decision making guides

Decision making guides simplify what can sometimes feel like an overwhelming process.

Checklists help customers compare providers using consistent criteria, such as experience, services, support, pricing transparency and previous work. They also encourage customers to focus on long term value rather than making decisions based solely on cost.

Together, these types of content help customers move through the consideration stage with greater confidence. Instead of relying on persuasive messaging alone, businesses can support informed decision making by providing practical resources that answer questions, explain options and make comparisons easier.

### How Businesses Can Build Trust During the Consideration Stage

Customers are looking for confidence rather than persuasion.

Businesses can support this process by providing clear and practical information.

Some of the most effective approaches include:

- Explaining services in straightforward language.
- Showing examples of previous work where appropriate.
- Publishing educational content that answers genuine customer questions.
- Being transparent about the process customers can expect.
- Including testimonials and client feedback.
- Making it easy for customers to ask further questions.

Whether a business provides website development, search engine optimisation, paid advertising, branding or AI automation, clarity often has a greater impact than lengthy promotional messaging.

### Common Mistakes Businesses Make During the Consideration Stage

Many businesses lose potential customers without realising why.

One common mistake is focusing entirely on themselves rather than addressing the customer's needs. Customers are interested in how a service solves their problem, not simply how long a business has existed.

Another mistake is providing too little information. If customers cannot understand what a service includes or how it works, they are likely to continue researching other providers.

Some businesses also overlook the importance of customer proof. Testimonials, portfolios and case studies provide valuable reassurance during the decision making process.

Finally, weak or outdated service pages can make comparison difficult. Customers should be able to understand a business's expertise without needing to request basic information.

### An Example of the Consideration Stage in Practice

Imagine a growing company that wants to generate more enquiries through its website.

After recognising that their current website is underperforming, they begin researching possible solutions. They compare website redesign services, SEO strategies, Google Ads campaigns and AI chatbots that can improve customer engagement.

They read articles, review agency websites, compare portfolios and examine testimonials. They also look for businesses that explain their process clearly and demonstrate experience across multiple digital marketing services.

After narrowing their options, they contact the businesses that have provided the clearest information and the strongest evidence of previous work.

The final decision is not based on price alone. It is based on confidence.

### Why the Consideration Stage Matters

The Consideration Stage of the Customer Journey is where customers decide which businesses deserve their attention.

Every blog article, service page, case study and testimonial contributes to that decision. Clear information helps customers compare their options with confidence, while incomplete or confusing content often encourages them to continue searching elsewhere.

Businesses that invest in educational content, transparent service information and a well organised website make it easier for potential customers to move towards a purchasing decision.

Whether someone is researching website design, SEO, digital marketing, AI automation or branding, they are looking for evidence that a business understands their objectives and can provide an appropriate solution.

By supporting customers during this stage instead of rushing them towards a sale, businesses are far more likely to build trust that lasts beyond the first enquiry.
