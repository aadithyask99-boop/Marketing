## The starting point

Bakwa had a product worth talking about. The Kerala-based brand makes bottles from plant-based materials, including sugarcane and corn starch, as an alternative to conventional plastic. The product had a clear point of difference. The challenge was getting the right people to notice it.

Sustainability is easy to talk about and harder to make practical. A good product does not create demand by itself. People had to understand what Bakwa was, why it was different and why it mattered to them. That became our job.

The budget was limited, so we had to decide where it would make the most difference. We could have started with a large website and put money into paid advertising. We didn't.

## We kept the website deliberately simple

With a limited budget, Bakwa did not need a large website at the beginning. So we built a single page with one enquiry form. Explain the product. Show what makes it different. Give people enough to decide. Make it easy to enquire. That was it.

The website was never meant to be the whole marketing strategy. It was there to support it. We wanted the budget and the effort to go into getting Bakwa in front of the right people, so our focus moved to content and social media.

## Finding the right position

The product already had a strong point of difference, so we did not need to manufacture a story. We needed to position it properly. Bakwa was not another company talking about sustainability. It had a physical alternative to plastic. That gave us a natural starting point:

- Sustainable packaging
- Alternatives to plastic
- Product innovation
- Environmental awareness
- Responsible consumption

The important part was deciding who needed to hear it. We focused on audiences and businesses with a genuine reason to care about sustainable packaging, rather than trying to make Bakwa relevant to everyone. Right place, right people, right time.

## Building the audience from zero

Instagram became an important part of the campaign. Rather than posting occasionally, we used it to build Bakwa's audience and tell the story consistently. In three months, <a href="https://www.instagram.com/bakwa.official" target="_blank" rel="noopener">Bakwa's Instagram</a> gained 5.3K+ followers organically. No follower campaigns. No paid growth.

The audience grew because the content reached people who were interested in what Bakwa was doing. That gave the brand something more useful than a number on a profile: people who had already shown an interest in the story.

## One simple post, a lot of business

One of the clearest examples was a static post. It was not a complicated campaign or an expensive production. It was a single poster. But it reached an audience far beyond Bakwa's existing followers and helped generate business enquiries. You can <a href="https://www.instagram.com/p/DbaVFQIDwcF/" target="_blank" rel="noopener">see the post on Instagram</a>.

The post reached 54,507 people, and only 5.5% were followers. For us, that was the number that mattered. The post was not speaking to people who already knew Bakwa. It was introducing the brand to people who had never met it, and that attention turned into enquiries and business.

## Then the story travelled

The next step was getting Bakwa's story beyond its own channels. We created authentic content that gave publications a real reason to talk about the brand, which led to coverage from major publications.

## One story became many

Once the story was out, it kept moving. Content around Bakwa was reposted and shared across 200+ web and social channels. The brand no longer relied on its own website or Instagram account to be discovered. People met Bakwa through publications, social posts, videos and other websites, and the more places it appeared, the more chances someone had to discover it, search for it and get in touch.

This is where building a brand differs from building backlinks. We were not chasing numbers for their own sake. We were creating reasons for people to talk about Bakwa.

The numbers tell one part of the story. The other part is where they came from: a single-page website, an enquiry form, consistent social content, a clear position, a product with a genuine point of difference and a story publications wanted to tell.

## Visibility beyond social media

As Bakwa started appearing across publications and social platforms, its online presence grew beyond its own channels, and the brand also gained visibility in Google's AI Overviews. That was another sign the work was building a broader digital footprint, not just sending traffic to one website. The aim was never to chase a particular search feature. It was to make Bakwa easier to discover and easier to understand wherever people look.

## Why it worked

We did not make it more complicated than it needed to be. The product had a real point of difference. The audience had a reason to care. The content had a story behind it. The website made it easy to enquire. And distribution took the story beyond Bakwa's own channels.

Most importantly, we did not spend money simply because we had a budget. We made choices.

## The next phase

The first phase was never about building the final version of Bakwa's digital presence. It was about proving what was possible, and it worked. In three months, a brand with a minimal website and no paid advertising built an audience, generated thousands of enquiries, earned coverage from major publications and generated more than ₹10 lakh in revenue.

Now the business has moved forward, and so has the website. We are working with Bakwa on its next one, including design and development. This time we have more to build on: we know what people responded to, which parts of the story attracted attention and which audiences engaged, and we know there is real demand behind the brand.

**The first website was built to get Bakwa noticed. The next one is being built for where the brand is going.**
