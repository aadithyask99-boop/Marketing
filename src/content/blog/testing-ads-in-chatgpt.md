On 9 February, the way we think about search and discovery online changed. OpenAI officially began [testing ads in ChatGPT](https://openai.com/index/testing-ads-in-chatgpt/), starting with adult users in the US on the Free and Go subscription tiers.

If you have been using ChatGPT to research business ideas, plan trips, or find services, you have probably noticed that it feels very different from a traditional search engine. It is a conversation. Now, that conversation is opening up to advertisers.

We have been following the reports from Adweek, TechCrunch, and NBC News to see how this is rolling out. While the test is currently limited, the implications for businesses of all sizes are massive.

Here is the breakdown of how testing ads in ChatGPT actually works and what you should be doing to prepare.

### What Do Ads In ChatGPT Actually Look Like?

The first thing to understand is that OpenAI is being very careful not to ruin the "chat" experience. According to their announcement, ads in ChatGPT do not influence the actual answers the AI gives you.

Instead, they appear as clearly labelled "sponsored" results, visually separated from the organic chat response. For example, if you are searching for recipe ideas, you might see a sponsored suggestion for a meal kit or a local grocery delivery service alongside the cooking tips.

The goal is relevance. The platform decides which ad to show by matching the topic of your conversation, your past chats, and your previous interactions with ads. If there are multiple businesses competing for your attention, OpenAI says they will select the one most relevant to your specific query.

### Who Sees Ads in ChatGPT, and Who Does Not

For now, this is a controlled test. Here is the current setup:

**Who sees them:** Logged-in adult users (18+) in the US on the Free and Go tiers.

**Who is skipped:** Users on Plus, Pro, Business, Enterprise, or Education tiers will remain ad-free.

**Safety first:** OpenAI has stated that ads will not appear near sensitive topics like health, mental health, or politics.

This phased rollout gives OpenAI time to listen to feedback and make sure the ads feel useful, not intrusive.

### The "Big Agency" Pilot: nicom, WPP, and Dentsu

While the test is in its early days, the "big players" are already lining up. Reporting from Adweek confirms that major global advertising groups like Omnicom, WPP, and Dentsu are participating in the testing ads in ChatGPT pilot.

**This tells us two things:**

There is significant confidence that conversational AI is the next major advertising frontier.

The early focus is likely on high-quality, brand-safe content.

However, OpenAI has made it clear that they see an opportunity for businesses of all sizes to be discovered in the moments when users are comparing ideas or working towards a decision.

### How Privacy Works in the ChatGPT Ads Pilot

One of the biggest concerns with testing ads in ChatGPT is privacy. We have all had that experience where we talk about something and then see an ad for it five minutes later on social media.

OpenAI is taking a different approach. Advertisers do not have access to your individual chats, chat history, or personal details. They only receive "aggregate" information, meaning they can see how many people clicked an ad, but they don't know who those people are.

Furthermore, users have a fair amount of control. You can:

Dismiss specific ads and share feedback.

See why you were shown a particular ad.

Delete your ad data with a single tap.

Manage your personalisation settings at any time.

Why this matters for your small business or startup

You might think, "I'm not in the US and I'm not a global brand, why should I care about testing ads in ChatGPT?"

The reason is simple: consumer behaviour is shifting. People are moving away from clicking through pages of blue links on Google and moving towards "Answer Engines."

If ChatGPT becomes a primary way people find services, your business needs to be ready. Even if you aren't running ads yet, the way the AI "recommends" a business depends on how clear and authoritative your online presence is.

### How To Prepare For The Rollout

Based on what we know about testing ads in ChatGPT, here is a practical checklist to ensure your business is ready when this goes global.

**1. Focus on conversational content**

Stop writing only for "keywords." Start answering the questions your customers actually ask. ChatGPT looks for information that fits naturally into a conversation. If your website has a clear, helpful FAQ section, you are already ahead.

**2. Tighten your local SEO**

OpenAI’s meal kit example proves that local relevance is key. Make sure your Google Business Profile and local directory listings are 100% accurate. If the AI is looking for a "best café near me" or a "marketing agency in Sevenoaks," your data needs to be easy for the bots to read.

**3. Get your tracking in order**

As with any new ad platform, those who have the best data will win. Ensure your Google Analytics 4 (GA4) and [conversion tracking](https://sproutsocial.com/glossary/conversion-tracking/) are set up properly now. When ads in ChatGPT become available to more businesses, you will need to know exactly which clicks are turning into customers.

**4. Build brand authority**

The AI is more likely to recommend (or show an ad for) a brand that is cited across the web. Work on getting real customer reviews, appearing in local news, and having a consistent message across all your social channels.
