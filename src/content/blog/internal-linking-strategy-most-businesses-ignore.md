Most websites publish pages then leave them disconnected. A blog post sits alone. A service page has no links pointing to it. A case study exists but nobody finds it. This is where an internal linking strategy most businesses ignore comes in.

Internal linking is one of the few SEO levers you fully control. It does not depend on other websites linking to you or trends changing. It is purely about how you structure your own site. Yet most businesses treat it as an afterthought, which costs them rankings, traffic, and enquiries.

This guide explains what an internal linking strategy actually is, why it matters, and how to build one that works.

## What Internal Linking Is and Why It Matters for SEO

### They Help Google Discover Pages

First, they help Google discover pages. [If a page has no links pointing to it, Google might never find it or crawl it regularly.](https://www.seerinteractive.com/insights/what-is-google-discover-how-do-you-optimize-for-it)

### They Help Distribute Authority

Second, they help distribute authority. Pages that receive more internal links are often treated as more important. If your homepage gets lots of authority from backlinks, internal links help pass some of that authority to your key service pages.

### They Help Google Understand Relationships

Third, they help Google understand relationships. If your blog post links to a service page, Google learns that the two topics are related.

For users, internal links are even simpler. They help people navigate and find relevant information. A good internal link structure makes the journey from a question to an answer to an enquiry smooth and obvious.

This is the foundation of why an internal linking strategy most businesses ignore is actually critical for both SEO and conversions.

## The Internal Linking Strategy Most Businesses Ignore

[There is a big difference between having internal links and having a strategy.](https://www.semrush.com/blog/internal-links/)

Most websites do one of two things. Either they have no real plan and links happen randomly as content is written. Or they only link from the homepage, creating a flat structure where everything is equally distant from the homepage.

A real strategy is different. It treats your website like a system where important pages get more authority and easier access. Service pages link to FAQs and case studies that support them. [Blog posts link back to the service they relate to.](/our-services/) Proof pages link to the enquiry page.

The goal is clear: make it easy for Google to find and value your important pages, and make it easy for visitors to move from discovery to action.

## Common Internal Linking Problems That Quietly Kill Performance

### Orphan Pages

[These are pages with no internal links pointing to them.](https://www.conductor.com/academy/what-are-orphan-pages-how-to-find-fix/) They might rank for something random, but Google does not treat them as important because nothing on your site references them. If you have a case study or a location page with no links to it, that is an orphan.

### Weak or Generic Anchor Text

Anchor text is the clickable words in a link. If every link just says "click here" or "read more", Google learns nothing about what the destination page is about. If links use specific language like "kitchen renovation case study" or "pricing for SEO services", Google and users both understand the context.

### Everything Links to the Homepage

Homepages rank for broad, competitive terms. Service pages rank for specific intent. If your only strategy is linking everything back to the homepage, you are not pushing authority to the pages that actually convert enquiries.

### Important Pages Are Buried Too Deep

If your top service page is 5 clicks away from the homepage, Google will crawl it less often and treat it as less important. Pages that matter should be easy to reach.

## A Simple Internal Link Structure That Works for Most Businesses

You do not need a complicated system. Most businesses can use a hub and spoke model.

### Core Pages

Core pages are your homepage, your main service pages, and your key location pages if you have them. These are the pages that rank and convert.

### Support Pages

Support pages are your FAQs, guides, blog posts, and educational content. These answer questions and help people decide.

### Proof Pages

Proof pages are your case studies, testimonials, and results. These build trust.

The strategy is simple. Support pages link to core pages. Proof pages link to core pages. Core pages link to each other when relevant. Visitors and Google both follow a natural path from learning about you to choosing you.

### Where to Add Internal Links for the Biggest Impact

You do not need to link everywhere. Focus on placements that matter.

### Inside Service Pages

Link to related services, your pricing guidance, relevant FAQs, and case studies that prove the service works. For example, [if you have a page about website redesign, link to your landing page optimisation service,](/our-services/) your SEO service, and your conversion rate optimisation work. A visitor reading about website redesign should easily find related services that support the main offer.

At Maximus, we link our website redesign page to our SEO and Content Creation service, our Digital Marketing and Advertising service, and our AI Automation solutions. This helps visitors understand the full picture and shows Google how these services relate.

### Inside Blog Posts

Every blog post should answer a question or explore a topic. At the end, link to the service that solves the problem the blog discusses. If you write about website speed, link to your Website Design and Development service. [If you write about lead capture, link to your AI, Automation and Chatbot Solutions service.](/our-services/)

For example, in our blog post about internal linking strategy, we could link to our SEO and Content Creation service since internal linking is a core SEO tactic. [We could also link to our Website Redesign and Optimisation service](/our-services/) since site structure is part of that work.

### Navigation and Footer

Navigation should be clear and focused. Your main services should be obvious. At Maximus, we list Website Design and Development, Digital Marketing and Advertising, SEO and Content Creation, Social Media Management, AI Automation and Chatbot Solutions, Email and SMS Marketing, and Brand and Identity [in our services menu.](/our-services/)

Footer can include links to key service pages, but keep it clean and avoid cluttering it with every page you have.

### Internal Linking Best Practices (Without Overcomplicating It)

A few simple rules keep your strategy clean.

Do not add more than 3 to 5 internal links per page. Too many links dilute the benefit and confuse visitors.

Use descriptive anchor text that describes the destination. Avoid "click here" or vague phrases.

Link to relevant pages only. Do not force links just because you want to link. If a page is not related, the link feels awkward and helps nobody.

Do not over-optimize. You are writing for humans first, Google second. A link should make sense in context.

### How to Audit Internal Links on Your Own Website

You do not need expensive tools to check your internal linking strategy.

Start with your top service pages. Look at each one and count how many other pages on your site link to it. If the number is low, you have found a priority.

Next, check for orphan pages. Search site:yourdomain.com in Google and look at your sitemap. [If you see pages in your sitemap that Google has not indexed or that have very low traffic, they might be orphans.](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/crawling-indexing/sitemaps/overview) Add internal links to them from relevant pages.

Finally, open a few of your blog posts. Do they link back to your service pages? Do they have a clear next step? If not, those are easy fixes.

If you want more detail, tools like Screaming Frog or Ahrefs show you the full link map, but a manual audit catches most issues.

### What to Fix First if You Want Faster SEO Gains

Prioritise by impact.

### First

Add internal links to your top converting service pages from related content. If a service page gets more internal links and better anchor text, it often ranks higher and converts better.

### Second

Find orphan pages and link to them. If you have content that nobody finds, a few strategic internal links can change that.

### Third

Build a simple hub structure. Pick 3 to 5 core topics your business is known for. Create a main page for each. Then create supporting content (blog posts, FAQs, guides) that links back to the main page. This shows Google you have depth in those areas.

### Fourth

Add internal links inside blog posts. Every piece of content should guide readers toward a relevant service or next step.
